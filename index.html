FDP Capstone · Case Study 13

AI agent for course management

Human-supervised support

A proposed course-support agent for routine FSWD questions. It helps students find approved resources, understand deadlines and track assignment milestones. It does not assign grades, change attendance, or make high-stakes academic decisions.

?

Student queries & FAQs

Responds from an instructor-approved knowledge base covering lab setup, experiment instructions, submission rules and common errors. Unanswered or uncertain questions are escalated to the course coordinator.

◷

Reminders & deadlines

Uses the course calendar to show upcoming lab milestones and deadlines. Real notifications require an approved calendar, email or messaging integration and student consent.

✓

Assignment tracking

Displays task status such as Not started, In progress, Submitted or Faculty review. Submission status is not a mark; faculty retain grading authority.

↗

Resource sharing

Recommends the relevant experiment, official documentation and instructor-approved examples based on the student's question and current learning objective.

FDP Capstone · Case Study 14

Research integration

Verify before formal citation

These research leads connect AI-supported education, automated assessment and learning analytics to the course-design project. Verify each paper's publisher record, authors, DOI and full text before formal submission.

2025 · Course outcomes & Bloom's taxonomy

Leveraging generative AI for course learning outcome categorization using Bloom's taxonomy

Omaima Almatrafi and Aditya Johri. Computers and Education: Artificial Intelligence, 8, 100404 (2025).

The study compared GPT-4 prompting strategies on 1,000 annotated learning outcomes. Context-rich, domain-aware prompting performed best among the prompt strategies, while a fine-tuned BERT model performed better overall. This supports using AI for a first-pass CO/Bloom tag, not as a replacement for faculty validation.

Read publisher article · DOI ↗
2024 · Assessment design

How Teachers Can Use Large Language Models and Bloom's Taxonomy to Create Educational Quizzes

Sabina Elkins, Ekaterina Kochmar, Jackie C. K. Cheung and Iulian Serban. Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence, 38(21), 23084–23091 (2024).

The paper studies LLM-generated quiz questions guided by Bloom-derived learning goals. In the reported experiments, teachers preferred quiz creation with generated questions, with no loss in quiz quality relative to handwritten versions and some quality measures improving. For FSWD, generated question drafts should still be reviewed for syllabus coverage, code accuracy and practical relevance.

Read AAAI proceedings · DOI ↗
2025 · Programming assessment

Integrating Generative AI into Programming Education: Student Perceptions and the Challenge of Correcting AI Errors

Dan Kohen-Vacs, Maya Usher and Marc Jansen. International Journal of Artificial Intelligence in Education, 35, 3166–3184 (2025).

Two quantitative studies examined student perceptions and performance on correcting LLM-generated code. Although students generally saw benefits, they had greater difficulty correcting AI-generated code than completing traditional instructor-designed tasks. This directly supports live debugging exercises, code explanation and viva as FSWD assessment evidence.

Read Springer Nature article · DOI ↗
2024 · Feedback for programming

Students' Perceptions and Preferences of Generative Artificial Intelligence Feedback for Programming

Zhengdong Zhang, Zihan Dong, Yang Shi, Thomas Price, Noboru Matsuda and Dongkuan Xu. Proceedings of the AAAI Conference on Artificial Intelligence, 38(21), 23250–23258 (2024).

The study explored ChatGPT-generated feedback for Java lab assignments. Students perceived the feedback as formative and preferred feedback conditioned on their code because it was more specific and corrective, while opinions on tone differed. For FSWD, feedback should point to testable improvements and retain instructor review, especially when student submissions contain private data.

Read AAAI proceedings · DOI ↗
2024 · Systematic mapping review

Generative AI in education and research: A systematic mapping review

Abdullahi Yusuf, Nasrin Pervin, Marcos Román-González and Norah Md Noor. Review of Education, 12(2), e3489 (2024).

This review mapped 407 publications and identified themes including pedagogical enhancement, adoption, performance, and ethical implications and risks. It also highlighted limited experimental evaluation and research gaps around cultural and ethical concerns. For this project, it supports a cautious pilot with transparent measures, human oversight and privacy safeguards rather than assuming that adding AI alone improves learning.

Read Wiley / Review of Education · DOI ↗

Proposed future research direction

Human-validated adaptive laboratory support for full-stack web development. Pilot whether formative quiz results, rubric evidence and learner reflections paired with tailored practice recommendations improve CO attainment. Use a comparison design where feasible, anonymise data, and obtain institutional approval before collecting research data.

Capture genuine screenshots for your FDP submission

  1. Open ChatGPT or the AI tool you actually used and submit the prompt above.
  2. Wait for its real response, then capture a screenshot showing both prompt and response. If it spans multiple screens, take more than one screenshot.
  3. On Windows, press Win + Shift + S to capture the actual tool window.
  4. Save the original screenshots and add them to your portfolio. Do not submit this website panel, a mock-up, or a generated image as proof of AI use.

Permitted support

  • Explain concepts and official documentation.
  • Brainstorm designs and test cases.
  • Request debugging hints and code review.
  • Improve comments, accessibility and documentation.

Disclosure & integrity

  • Disclose the AI tool and how it was used.
  • Cite sources and verify generated references.
  • Never fabricate results, logs, attendance or research.
  • Do not present AI output as independent work when disclosure is required.

Privacy & security

  • Do not send personal student data, passwords, tokens or private records to public AI tools.
  • Use approved services and minimum necessary data.
  • Inspect generated code for unsafe input handling and dependencies.
  • Never commit API keys or database credentials to GitHub.

Faculty oversight

  • Verify syllabus fit, correctness, accessibility and fairness.
  • AI suggestions do not determine grades or attendance.
  • Provide a way to question automated suggestions.
  • Follow institutional policy if stricter.

AI-resilient assessment strategy

  1. Process evidence: commits, design notes, tests and debugging records.
  2. Live practical: each student implements or modifies a feature under supervision.
  3. Viva: explain code, trade-offs, errors and security decisions.
  4. Individual variation: equivalent parameterised tasks require personal reasoning.
  5. Transparent rubric: correctness, code quality, testing, explanation and AI disclosure.
  6. Fair process: do not rely on AI detector scores alone as proof of misconduct.

Academic note: This is a proposed course-level policy. Research leads are not five verified citations; replace them with papers whose exact title, authors, venue and DOI have been checked.
